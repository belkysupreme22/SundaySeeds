## Audience and purpose

Proposed audience: adults beginning or renewing a Christian study practice, including learners unfamiliar with Bible study. No prior course or assessed knowledge is proposed as a prerequisite. The intended reading level, accessibility and theological framing require content review; these have not been validated with learners.

The course should help a learner explain four biblical teachings and identify small, responsible ways to practise them. It must not grade faith, spiritual maturity, prayerfulness or personal worth. Knowledge checks assess understanding of the presented material; reflections invite personal application.

Existing course ID: `everyday-faith`. Keep the current IDs when reviewing this candidate so existing guest progress remains understandable. Any future connected enrollment and progress require their own context; importing this content must not silently convert guest activity into account history.

## Course outcomes and outline

By the end, a learner should be able to:

- Describe the Lord’s Prayer as a pattern for sincere prayer and identify a realistic moment to pray.
- Explain the mustard-seed image of God’s kingdom and identify a small practice or welcoming action.
- Describe David’s preparation and distinguish responsible courage from ignoring risk.
- Explain the Samaritan’s practical care and identify a respectful way to help a neighbour.

The explanation outcomes can be checked automatically. A learner’s proposed actions are invitations to practise, not proof of spiritual growth or required disclosures.

## Lesson activity specification

### 1. A moment with God

- Teaching sequence: sincere prayer → the Lord’s Prayer as a pattern → returning to prayer during the day.
- Existing reflection: consider something to be thankful for and a concern to bring honestly to God.
- Existing practice: connect a phrase of the Lord’s Prayer to a real need, relationship or decision; choose an existing daily rhythm as a reminder to pray.
- Existing correct-answer concepts: `prayer-1` sincere prayer rather than performance; `prayer-2` daily needs and forgiveness; `prayer-3` a regular moment for honest prayer.
- Memory verse: “Pray without ceasing.” — 1 Thessalonians 5:17, labelled KJV in source. Do not imply prayer guarantees a desired outcome.

### 2. Small seeds of faith

- Teaching sequence: small beginnings → patient growth → making room for others.
- Existing reflection: recall a relationship, habit or act of care that began quietly and grew over time.
- Existing practice: choose a small, realistic practice and consider an invitation, listening or welcome that could help someone belong.
- Existing correct-answer concepts: `faith-1` God’s kingdom; `faith-2` small beginnings can matter; `faith-3` welcoming someone who feels left out.
- Memory verse: “For we walk by faith, not by sight.” — 2 Corinthians 5:7, labelled KJV in source. Review its relationship to the main passage; do not turn the parable into a promise of personal success.

### 3. Courage over fear

- Teaching sequence: name a challenge → recognise existing preparation → choose wise, supported courage.
- Existing reflection: consider an avoided conversation, responsibility or decision and the support it may require.
- Existing practice: identify a responsible next step, which may include asking for advice, help or appropriate boundaries.
- Existing correct-answer concepts: `courage-1` caring for sheep; `courage-2` familiar skills and tools; `courage-3` asking for help.
- Memory verse: “What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.” — Psalm 56:3, labelled KJV in source. Preserve the distinction between trust and denying risk.

### 4. Love your neighbour

- Teaching sequence: notice a person → offer practical care → act as a neighbour across familiar boundaries.
- Existing reflection: consider someone who may be overlooked at work, in the neighbourhood or at church.
- Existing practice: listen before assuming what is needed; choose respectful help and involve someone better placed to assist when appropriate.
- Existing correct-answer concepts: `kindness-1` the Samaritan; `kindness-2` practical care; `kindness-3` listening and respectful help.
- Memory verse: “Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.” — Matthew 22:39, labelled KJV excerpt in source. Distinguish this supporting verse from the lesson’s main passage.

These reflections and practices already exist as authored prompts. This blueprint does not introduce submitted answers, stored personal reflections, teacher access to private responses or proof-of-practice requirements.

## Accepted first-course completion — B1-08 subset

- Accepted required work: finish all four lesson knowledge checks, with retries and answer explanations available. No pass mark is required for this first course; finishing a check and answering every item correctly are different facts.
- Track reading position or reading completion separately from check completion. Opening a lesson must not imply it was read or understood.
- Reflections, memory practice and real-life actions are optional and ungraded. Do not infer completion, attendance or spiritual growth from them.
- Completion wording must explain the chosen rule. No certificate, graded qualification or prerequisite unlock is included in this candidate.

The owner accepted this rule. Trusted attempt/score/retry-limit/reporting semantics still need definition before connected completion is implemented; source planning is not a working progress service.

## Two journeys using the same content

**Personal study:** browse the course, see its audience, outcomes, four lessons and time basis, then join after verified sign-in. Work through the suggested sequence at a chosen pace and return to unfinished work. Whether later lessons require earlier completion remains a product decision; this blueprint recommends open lesson access.

**Optional group study:** propose four meetings, one lesson per meeting, with personal reading beforehand and a facilitator-led discussion of the existing prompts. The class supplies invitations, dates and facilitation; those are not part of the reusable course. A late joiner can see the upcoming lesson without receiving invented attendance or earlier completions. No group schedule or connected class exists merely because this blueprint describes one.

Suggested discovery metadata for review: adult audience; introductory familiarity; Prayer, Faith, Courage and Kindness topics; goals of developing a study rhythm and applying biblical teaching in daily life. These are proposed course tags, not learner diagnoses or guaranteed outcomes. Use profile answers as optional recommendation signals; allow learners to browse the whole available catalog.

That comment is not a completed rights or publication review. Named people and an accountable publishing organisation are still unassigned.

Content review checklist:

- Read the full main passages; check each explanation, question, distractor and answer explanation for accuracy and ambiguity.
- Review the connection between each memory verse and its main passage; verify exact quotation, reference, translation label and required attribution.
- Confirm audience, terminology, theological framing, respectful applications and responsible treatment of fear/help-seeking.
- Review reading level, understandable instructions and alternatives for learners who cannot complete a suggested real-life action.
- Confirm reflection remains optional and private unless a separate, explicit sharing flow is designed and approved.
- Validate the estimated reading time with readers; state additional study effort honestly.
- Assign the named roles above and resolve content ownership/Scripture permissions before teaching-content import or production publication.

## Edition example — joined edition accepted

Suppose a learner joins edition 1 with four required checks and finishes two. Adding two required lessons to the same live course would change 2/4 (50%) into 2/6 (33%). Accepted default: keep that enrollment on its stable edition for material curriculum changes and offer an explicit choice to move. This accepts the joined-edition subset of B1-07; it does not define progress transfer.

Minor corrections and urgent rights/safety withdrawals need an explicit correction policy; stable editions must not guarantee continued access to withdrawn material. Accountable content roles and the remaining correction/transfer/access rules still need resolution before their publication/access operations are enabled.
